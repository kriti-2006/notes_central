## Ruby laser (a three-level solid-state laser)

- **Active medium:** a ruby rod, Al₂O₃ doped with Cr³⁺ ions.
- **Pumping:** a xenon flash lamp (optical pumping).
- **Output:** 694.3 nm (red), as **pulses**.
- **Limitation:** a three-level system needs intense pumping, so it cannot produce a continuous beam easily.

## He-Ne laser (a four-level gas laser)

- **Active medium:** a mixture of helium and neon in a ratio of about 10 : 1.
- **Pumping:** electrical discharge. Helium atoms are excited and pass their energy to neon atoms through collisions.
- **Output:** 632.8 nm (red), as a **continuous** beam.
- **Advantage:** a four-level system reaches population inversion more easily than a three-level one.

## Semiconductor (diode) laser

- Light is produced at a forward-biased **p-n junction**, usually made of GaAs.
- It is small, efficient and cheap.
- It is used in optical fibre communication, barcode scanners and laser pointers.

## Comparison

| Feature | Ruby | He-Ne | Semiconductor |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Type | Solid state | Gas | Semiconductor |
| Energy levels | Three | Four | Bands |
| Output | Pulsed | Continuous | Continuous or pulsed |
| Wavelength | 694.3 nm | 632.8 nm | Depends on the material |

## Applications of lasers

- **Industry:** cutting, welding and drilling metals.
- **Medicine:** eye surgery (LASIK), removing tumours, dermatology.
- **Communication:** a light source for optical fibres.
- **Science:** holography, spectroscopy, measuring distances (LIDAR).
- **Everyday use:** barcode scanners, laser printers, optical discs.

> **Revision tip:** Learn the energy-level diagram for both the ruby and He-Ne lasers. Diagram questions are common.
