## Features of technical writing

Technical writing explains complex information to a specific audience so that they can **understand it or act on it**.

- **Accurate:** facts, figures and units must be correct.
- **Clear:** use simple sentences and define every technical term.
- **Concise:** remove words that add no meaning.
- **Objective:** avoid personal opinions and emotional language.
- **Well structured:** use headings, numbered lists, tables and figures.

## Technical reports

### Common structure

1. Title page
2. Abstract or executive summary
3. Table of contents
4. Introduction: purpose and scope
5. Methodology
6. Findings or results
7. Conclusions
8. Recommendations
9. References and appendices

**Informal reports** (memos, progress reports) are short. **Formal reports** (project or feasibility reports) follow the full structure above.

## Business letters

The parts of a formal letter, in order:

- Sender's address and date
- Receiver's name and address
- Subject line
- Salutation, for example *Dear Sir/Madam*
- Body: introduction, details, closing request
- Complimentary close, for example *Yours faithfully*
- Signature, name and designation

Use **block format**, where everything is aligned left, for most modern letters.

## Professional emails

- Write a **specific subject line**, for example *Request for lab slot: 12 March*.
- Use one main idea per email and short paragraphs.
- Put the action you need, and its deadline, near the top.
- Use **Reply all** only when everyone needs the reply.
- Proofread before sending, and mention attachments in the email body.

## Resume vs CV

| Resume | Curriculum vitae (CV) |
| --- | --- |
| 1–2 pages | Can be longer |
| Tailored to each job | A full academic and work history |
| Focuses on skills | Focuses on detail and achievements |

> **Tip:** In reports, write in the passive or third person ("The experiment was conducted…") unless your institution asks otherwise.
