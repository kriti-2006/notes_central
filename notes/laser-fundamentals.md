## What is a laser?

**LASER** stands for **Light Amplification by Stimulated Emission of Radiation**. Laser light is:

- **Monochromatic:** a single, very narrow range of wavelengths.
- **Coherent:** all the waves are in phase.
- **Highly directional:** the beam spreads very little.
- **Intense:** a lot of energy is concentrated in a small area.

## Interaction of light with matter

Consider an atom with energy levels E₁ (lower) and E₂ (upper). A photon of energy hν = E₂ − E₁ can cause three processes:

1. **Absorption:** the atom absorbs the photon and moves up from E₁ to E₂.
2. **Spontaneous emission:** an excited atom falls back to E₁ on its own and emits a photon in a random direction and phase.
3. **Stimulated emission:** an incoming photon triggers an excited atom to fall to E₁. The atom emits a second photon that is **identical** to the first in frequency, phase and direction. This is the process that amplifies light in a laser.

## Population inversion

Normally there are more atoms in the lower level than in the upper one (N₁ > N₂). Lasing needs the reverse, **N₂ > N₁**, which is called **population inversion**.

- **Pumping** is the process that creates population inversion. It can be optical, using a flash lamp; electrical discharge; or chemical.
- A **metastable state** is an excited state with a relatively long lifetime, around 10⁻³ s. Atoms collect there, which makes population inversion possible.

## Einstein's coefficients

- A₂₁: the coefficient of spontaneous emission
- B₁₂: the coefficient of absorption
- B₂₁: the coefficient of stimulated emission

At equilibrium B₁₂ = B₂₁, and the ratio A₂₁ / B₂₁ is proportional to ν³. This means spontaneous emission dominates at high frequencies, which makes X-ray lasers difficult to build.

## Components of a laser

| Component | Role |
| --- | --- |
| Active medium | The material that lases, such as a ruby crystal or a He-Ne gas mixture |
| Pumping source | Supplies energy to create population inversion |
| Optical resonator | Two mirrors, one fully and one partially reflecting, that bounce photons back and forth to amplify them |

> **Exam tip:** Be ready to compare spontaneous and stimulated emission point by point: direction, phase, coherence, and whether an external photon is needed.
